=====================================================================
PART 1: WHAT IS THIS APPLICATION?
=====================================================================

It is a private website (an "intranet") for your estate agency.

Think of it like a secure clubhouse on the internet:
  - Only people with a company email address can get in the door.
  - Once inside, each agent sees THEIR OWN leases, billing,
    maintenance requests, deposit payouts and renewals - and nobody
    else's.
  - There are six big buttons for submitting new requests, which
    open the same Google Forms your agents already use today.

THE MOST IMPORTANT IDEA:

Nothing about how your admin team works changes. Your admins keep
updating the same five Google Sheets (the "workflow trackers") they
use today. The new website simply READS those sheets and shows each
agent a tidy, phone-friendly view of their own rows.

Picture it like a restaurant:
  - The Google Sheets are the kitchen, where the admins do the work.
  - The new website is the serving window, where each agent can look
    in and see how their own order is coming along.
  - The website never cooks anything itself. It only looks.

That means: if the website ever breaks, your business keeps running
exactly as before. Zero risk to your existing process.

PART 2: THE TECHNOLOGY, EXPLAINED LIKE YOU'RE 10
=====================================================================

The application is built from a few well-known pieces of technology.
You don't need to understand them deeply, but here's what each one
is, using everyday comparisons:

NEXT.JS (say: "next jay-ess")
  This is the framework the website is built with - think of it as
  the pre-built skeleton of a house. Instead of building walls and
  plumbing from scratch, builders start with a proven frame. It's
  made by a big company (Vercel) and used by huge websites like
  Netflix and TikTok. It handles both the part you SEE (the pages)
  and the part you DON'T see (the behind-the-scenes work).

TYPESCRIPT
  The language the code is written in. It's like writing with
  spell-check turned on - the computer catches mistakes before they
  become bugs that users see.

GOOGLE SIGN-IN (the login system)
  Instead of inventing our own usernames and passwords (risky and
  annoying), agents log in with the Google account they already
  have for their company email. It's like using your office access
  card to open a new door - no new keys to hand out, and when
  someone leaves the company and their email is switched off, they
  automatically lose access to the intranet too.

THE "SERVICE ACCOUNT" (the robot reader)
  This is a special robot Google account that the website uses to
  read your five tracker spreadsheets. You share the spreadsheets
  with the robot the same way you'd share them with a person - but
  the robot can ONLY LOOK, never edit. It's like giving someone a
  window to look through, not a key to the room.

THE DATABASE (a small filing cabinet)
  The website keeps one small filing cabinet of its own. It does
  NOT store your leases or requests (those stay in Google Sheets).
  It only stores three small things:
    - Who is allowed to log in
    - "Also known as" names (explained in Part 9)
    - A logbook of who logged in and when

TAILWIND CSS (the paint and decorating kit)
  The tool used to make everything look good and work nicely on
  phones. All your brand colours live in ONE place, like a single
  tin of paint - change the tin, repaint the whole house.

THE 60-SECOND MEMORY (why it stays fast)
  When one agent looks at the leases page, the website fetches the
  sheet and remembers it for 60 seconds. If 50 more agents look in
  that same minute, they all get the remembered copy instantly.
  This keeps the site fast and keeps Google happy, even with 130+
  agents using it at once.

  1. A computer (Windows or Mac) to do the one-time setup on.

  2. NODE.JS - a free program that runs the website's code.
     Think of it as the engine the website runs on.

terminal (on Windows it's called "PowerShell"). Don't be scared of
it - it's just a way of giving the computer typed instructions
instead of mouse clicks.

To open it on Windows:
  - Press the Windows key, type: powershell , press Enter.

To "go into" the project folder, type this and press Enter:
  cd "C:\Docs\Refilwe Mabotja\remax-one-intranet"

(If the folder is somewhere else, put that path in the quotes
instead.) Every command in this guide is typed AFTER doing this.


=====================================================================
PART 4: ONE-TIME GOOGLE SETUP (ABOUT 15 MINUTES)
=====================================================================

This creates two things: the LOGIN KEY (so agents can sign in with
their company email) and the ROBOT READER (so the website can read
your sheets). You only ever do this once.

Go to https://console.cloud.google.com and sign in with a company
Google account.

STEP A - CREATE A PROJECT
  At the top of the page, click the project dropdown, then
  "New Project". Name it something like "remax-one-intranet" and
  click Create. Make sure it's selected afterwards.

STEP B - SWITCH ON THE SHEETS API
  In the search bar at the top, type "Google Sheets API", click it,
  then click the blue ENABLE button. This flips the switch that
  allows reading spreadsheets.

STEP C - THE CONSENT SCREEN (the login "front door" settings)
  Search for "OAuth consent screen".
  Choose "Internal" - THIS IS IMPORTANT. "Internal" means ONLY
  people in your company's Google Workspace can ever log in.
  Fill in the app name (e.g. "One Rentals Intranet") and a support
  email. Save.

STEP D - CREATE THE LOGIN KEY
  Search for "Credentials", click "+ Create credentials", choose
  "OAuth client ID", then application type "Web application".
  Fill in these two boxes:

    Authorized JavaScript origins:
      http://localhost:3000

    Authorized redirect URIs:
      http://localhost:3000/api/auth/callback/google

  ("localhost:3000" means "this website running on my own computer"
  - it's how we test before going live. In Part 6 you'll come back
  and add the real website address here too.)

  Click Create. A box pops up showing a CLIENT ID and a CLIENT
  SECRET. Copy both somewhere safe - you'll paste them in Part 5.
  Treat the secret like a password.

STEP E - CREATE THE ROBOT READER
  Search for "Service Accounts", click "+ Create service account".
  Name it "intranet-reader". Skip the optional permission steps
  (click Done).

  Now click on the account you just made, go to the "Keys" tab,
  click "Add key" > "Create new key" > choose JSON > Create.
  A file downloads to your computer. This file is the robot's ID
  card - keep it private, never email it around.

  Open that file with Notepad. You'll see lines like:
    "client_email": "intranet-reader@....iam.gserviceaccount.com"
    "private_key": "-----BEGIN PRIVATE KEY-----\n....."
  You'll copy these two values in Part 5.

STEP F - INTRODUCE THE ROBOT TO YOUR SPREADSHEETS
  Open each of the SEVEN tracker spreadsheets (leases, billing,
  maintenance, deposit payouts, renewals, queries, POPs). On each:
    - Click the green "Share" button
    - Paste in the robot's email address (the client_email above)
    - Set it to "Viewer"
    - Untick "Notify people", click Share/Send.
  Now the robot can look at (but never change) all seven sheets.

  DON'T SKIP THE LAST TWO. The queries and POP trackers were added
  after the first five. If you forget to share them, "My Queries"
  and "My POPs" will show a red "couldn't load" message while every
  other page works perfectly - which is a confusing thing to debug.


=====================================================================
PART 5: SETTING UP THE APPLICATION ON YOUR COMPUTER
=====================================================================

STEP 1 - INSTALL THE APP'S BUILDING BLOCKS
  Open PowerShell, go into the project folder (see Part 3), then
  type:

    npm install

  This downloads all the ready-made parts the app is built from.
  It takes a few minutes. Lots of text scrolling by is normal.

STEP 2 - FILL IN THE SETTINGS FILE
  The app reads its private settings (keys, passwords) from a file
  called ".env". There's a template ready for you:

  In the project folder, find the file called  .env.example ,
  make a COPY of it, and rename the copy to exactly:  .env

  Open .env with Notepad. Every line that looks like
  NAME="value" is a setting. Fill these in:

    NEXTAUTH_URL
      Leave as http://localhost:3000 for now.

    NEXTAUTH_SECRET
      A long random scramble of letters and numbers (30+ characters)
      that the app uses to seal login sessions - like the unique
      pattern on a wax seal. Mash the keyboard or use a password
      generator. Never share it.

    GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID and GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET
      Paste the two values from Part 4, Step D.

    ALLOWED_EMAIL_DOMAINS
      Your company's email domain - the part after the @ in staff
      emails. E.g. if staff emails end in @remax-one.co.za, put:
      ALLOWED_EMAIL_DOMAINS="remax-one.co.za"
      This is the bouncer at the door: any email NOT ending in this
      is turned away, even if it's a real Google account.

    GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_EMAIL
      The robot's email (client_email from the JSON file, Part 4
      Step E).

    GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_PRIVATE_KEY
      The robot's private_key from the same JSON file. Copy the
      WHOLE thing, including the -----BEGIN and -----END parts and
      all the \n bits, keeping it on one line inside the quotes.
      This one is fiddly - copy it exactly as it appears in the
      JSON file.

    DATABASE_URL
      Leave as it is ( file:./dev.db ) - that just means "keep the
      little filing cabinet in a local file".

STEP 3 - CREATE THE FILING CABINET (DATABASE)
  In PowerShell, in the project folder, type:

    npm run db:migrate

  When it asks for a name for the change, just type: init
  and press Enter. This builds the small database file.

STEP 4 - START THE APP AND TEST IT
  Type:

    npm run dev

  Wait until it says "Ready". Now open your web browser and go to:

    http://localhost:3000

  You should see the login page with the logo. Click "Sign in with
  company email", pick your company Google account, and you should
  land on the dashboard with the six submit buttons and the menu on
  the left.

  QUICK HEALTH CHECK: visit  http://localhost:3000/api/health
  If it says "ok": true - everything is wired up correctly.
  If it lists names under "missingEnv", those settings in your .env
  file are empty or misspelled.

  To stop the app, click in the PowerShell window and press
  Ctrl + C.


=====================================================================
PART 6: PUTTING IT ON THE INTERNET (DEPLOYMENT)
=====================================================================

Running on your computer is just for testing - when you close your
laptop, the site goes off. To make it available to all 130+ agents,
24/7, you put it on a hosting service. Think of hosting as renting
a shop in a mall instead of selling from your garage.

THE EASY, RECOMMENDED WAY: VERCEL
Vercel is the company that makes Next.js, and hosting there is
mostly clicking buttons. The general idea:

  1. Put the project code into a free GitHub account (GitHub is
     like Google Drive, but for code).

  2. Sign up at vercel.com (you can sign in with GitHub), click
     "Add New Project", and pick your project. Vercel builds and
     publishes it automatically.

  3. Give Vercel your settings: in the Vercel project's
     Settings > Environment Variables, add every setting from your
     .env file, one by one. Two of them CHANGE for the live site:

       NEXTAUTH_URL = your real address, e.g.
                      https://portal.remax-one.co.za
                      (or the address Vercel gives you, like
                      https://remax-one-intranet.vercel.app)

       DATABASE_URL = a "grown-up" database. The local file version
                      is fine for testing, but for the live site use
                      a free managed Postgres database (Vercel
                      Postgres, Neon, or Supabase - all have free
                      tiers and give you a DATABASE_URL to paste).
                      One small code change goes with this: in the
                      file prisma/schema.prisma, the line
                      provider = "sqlite" must become
                      provider = "postgresql". Then run, one time:
                      npm run db:deploy

  4. Tell Google about the new address: go back to Google Cloud >
     Credentials > your OAuth client (Part 4, Step D) and ADD the
     live versions alongside the localhost ones:

       Origins:       https://your-real-address
       Redirect URIs: https://your-real-address/api/auth/callback/google

     (Without this, Google refuses logins from the live site.)

  5. Visit https://your-real-address/api/health and check it says
     "ok": true. Then log in and test each menu page.

  6. (Optional but nice) In Vercel > Settings > Domains you can
     attach your own web address like portal.remax-one.co.za.

BEFORE YOU ANNOUNCE IT TO THE AGENTS - FINAL CHECKLIST:
  [ ] ALLOWED_EMAIL_DOMAINS is set on the live site (without it,
      the live site refuses ALL logins on purpose, for safety)
  [ ] NEXTAUTH_URL is the real https address
  [ ] The live address was added in Google Cloud (step 4 above)
  [ ] All five sheets are shared with the robot reader
  [ ] The health check page says ok: true
  [ ] The agent-name columns are verified (see Part 9 - important!)
  [ ] Your real logo and colours are in (see Part 8)

PART 7: HOW AGENTS USE IT DAY-TO-DAY
=====================================================================

  1. Go to the intranet address on any phone, tablet or computer.
  2. Tap "Sign in with company email" and pick your work Google
     account. (Personal Gmail accounts are politely refused.)
  3. You land on the HOME page:
       - Eight big buttons: Submit New Lease, Submit Billing, Submit
         Lease Renewal, Submit Deposit Payout Request, Submit
         Maintenance, Submit RedRabbit Profile Request, Submit a
         Query, Submit a POP. Tapping one opens the familiar Google
         Form in a new tab - fill it in exactly as before.
       - A menu on the left (on a phone: tap the three-line button
         in the top corner to open it) with:
           My Leases            - every lease you've submitted, with
                                  date, property, admin, status,
                                  invoices, recon, outstanding docs
           My Billing           - your billing instructions & status
           My Maintenance       - your maintenance requests & notes
           My Deposit Payouts   - capturing and payout status
           My Renewals          - scope, instructions and status
           My Queries           - every query you've raised
           My POPs              - every proof of payment you've sent
  4. On every list page you can:
       - SEARCH (type a property name, a status, an admin's name)
       - REFRESH (pull the newest info from the tracker)
       - Read the colour-coded status pills at a glance:
         green = done/complete, orange = busy/pending, red = a
         problem or something outstanding.
  5. Tap "Sign out" (top right) when done - especially on shared
     computers.

Statuses shown come straight from the admin team's tracker sheets.
When an admin updates the sheet, agents see it within about a
minute.


=====================================================================
PART 8: CHANGING THE LOOK AND FEEL (BRANDING)
=====================================================================

Everything about the look lives in just three places:

  1. THE LOGO
     Replace the file  public\logo.svg  with your official logo
     file, keeping the same file name. (If your logo is a .png,
     put it in the public folder and change "logoSrc" in the file
     src\config\brand.ts to "/your-logo.png".)

  2. THE COLOURS AND FONT
     Open  src\app\globals.css  in Notepad. Right at the top is a
     clearly-marked section like:

       --brand-primary: #232323;     (buttons and highlights)
       --brand-secondary: #c4c6c8;   (the menu background)
       --brand-heading: #232323;     (page titles)
       --brand-sidebar-ink: #232323; (the writing ON the menu)

     Those #-codes are colour codes. Swap in your own brand codes
     (your marketing team or a site like htmlcolorcodes.com can
     give you the code for any colour). Change the code, save, and
     the ENTIRE site repaints itself.

     THE ONE RULE: every background has a matching "ink" (writing)
     colour, and the two must be far apart - dark writing on a
     light background or light writing on a dark one. If you make
     the menu (--brand-secondary) dark, change --brand-sidebar-ink
     and --brand-sidebar-muted to something light, or the menu
     words disappear. Page titles (--brand-heading) always sit on
     white, so keep that one dark.

     A NOTE ON FONTS: a font only shows if it lives on the reader's
     phone or computer. Gotham does not, so the site falls back to
     the next font in the list. Embedding Gotham properly needs
     the licensed font files - ask before buying them.

  3. THE NAMES
     Open  src\config\brand.ts  and change the company name,
     tagline and support email text.

After changing these on the live site, the hosting service rebuilds
and the new look appears in a minute or two.

PART 9: THE ONE IMPORTANT THING TO CHECK BEFORE LAUNCH
=====================================================================

THE "WHICH COLUMN HAS THE AGENT'S NAME?" QUESTION

The website shows each agent only THEIR rows. To do that, it looks
at the column in each tracker sheet that contains the agent's name,
and matches it to the person who logged in.

The original requirements listed every column EXCEPT that one. So
the app currently uses best guesses:

    Leases sheet:       column C
    Billing sheet:      column B
    Maintenance sheet:  column B
    Deposit sheet:      column C
    Renewals sheet:     column B

PLEASE CHECK: open each tracker sheet and look at the header row.
Find which column actually holds the agent's name. If a guess is
wrong, fix it by adding a line to your .env file (or the hosting
service's environment variables), for example:

    SHEETS_LEASES_AGENT_COL="D"

(There's one for each: SHEETS_BILLING_AGENT_COL,
SHEETS_MAINTENANCE_AGENT_COL, SHEETS_DEPOSITS_AGENT_COL,
SHEETS_RENEWALS_AGENT_COL.)

THE QUERIES AND POP SHEETS WORK DIFFERENTLY

Those two aren't guessed at all. The app opens the sheet, reads the
header row, and works out for itself which column holds the agent -
preferring an "Agent email address" column, because an email is an
exact match and a typed name never quite is. It also builds the
table columns from those same headers, so whatever the admins have
in the sheet is what agents see.

Two things follow from that:

  - If admins use a column for private remarks, agents WILL see it
    on their own rows. Say the word and that column gets hidden.
  - If the app cannot find an agent column at all, it deliberately
    shows NOTHING and says so on screen. It will never fall back to
    showing everybody's rows. If you see that message, add a line
    like SHEETS_POPS_AGENT_COL="B" to your .env, using the column
    letter that holds the agent's name or email.

THE "ALSO KNOWN AS" NAMES (ALIASES)

Agents log in with an EMAIL, but the sheets contain NAMES typed by
humans - and humans are inconsistent. The sheet might say
"J. Smith" one month and "John Smith" the next, while his Google
account says "Johnathan Smith".

If an agent logs in and sees an empty list even though they have
rows in the sheet, this mismatch is almost always why. The fix:
add their alternative spellings ("aliases") to the app's filing
cabinet. In PowerShell, in the project folder, type:

    npx prisma studio

A friendly page opens in your browser showing the database like a
spreadsheet. Open "AgentProfile", find the agent (they appear here
automatically after their first login), then add rows in
"AgentAlias" with the exact spellings used in the sheets. Done -
their rows appear on their next refresh.

MAKING SOMEONE A MANAGER (ADMIN)

In that same Prisma Studio page, changing a person's "role" from
AGENT to ADMIN makes them see EVERYONE's rows in every list -
useful for the rentals manager and admin staff.

REMOVING SOMEONE'S ACCESS

Two layers of protection:
  - When IT switches off their company Google account, they can't
    log in anymore - automatically.
  - You can also flip their "active" switch to false in Prisma
    Studio to block them instantly, even if their email still works.

PART 10: COMMON PROBLEMS AND FIXES
=====================================================================

"Please sign in with your company email address"
  -> They used a personal Gmail or an email whose domain isn't in
     ALLOWED_EMAIL_DOMAINS. Or their profile was set to inactive.

A page says "Couldn't load your leases" (or similar)
  -> Usually one of:
     - That sheet wasn't shared with the robot reader (Part 4 F)
     - The Sheets API wasn't enabled (Part 4 B)
     - The robot's private key was pasted incorrectly into .env -
       recopy it carefully from the JSON file.

An agent's list is empty but their rows ARE in the sheet
  -> The name-matching issue. See Part 9: check the agent-name
     column guesses, and add aliases for that agent.

Error mentioning "Tab with gid ... not found"
  -> Someone deleted and recreated a tab in a tracker spreadsheet,
     which gives it a new internal number. A developer updates one
     number in src\config\sheets.ts.

Login goes round in circles
  -> NEXTAUTH_URL doesn't match the address in the browser's
     address bar. Make them match.

The site looks broken after a colour change
  -> A typo in globals.css - colour codes must look like #1a2b3c
     and each line must end with a semicolon ;

WHEN IN DOUBT: visit  /api/health  on the site (for example
https://your-address/api/health). It tells you if any settings are
missing, by name. It never reveals the secret values themselves.
